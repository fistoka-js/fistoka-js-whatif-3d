Why does ice float, when most solids sink in their own liquid? Drop a block of solid wax into melted wax, and it sinks straight to the bottom. But ice bobs right back up. The secret is the water molecule. It is shaped like a tiny boomerang: one oxygen, two hydrogens. In liquid water, these molecules tumble around and pack closely together. But when water freezes, they lock into a neat hexagon pattern, with empty space in the middle. That open pattern makes ice about nine percent less dense than water. So it floats, and most of an iceberg hides below the surface. That matters for life. Lakes freeze from the top down, and the ice works like a lid, keeping fish alive in the water underneath.
